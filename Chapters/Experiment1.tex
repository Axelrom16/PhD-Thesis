\chapter{Unconditional Flow Matching Model for X-Ray Images}\label{ch:exp1}
%************************************************

Most of the recent techniques, as can be seen in the literature review part, inlcude some extra components 
or modification to original pre-trained generative models to achieve better performance with an improvement 
in interpretability (adding diesentanging capabilities in the latent space) and efficiently. This pre-trained
models are trained on large datasets and are not trained on medical data, which is the domain of interest for 
this thesis. Because of that, I decided to train an unconditional flow matching model with a medical 
dataset to have a pre-trained model to use for further experiments. \\
\\
I used the NIH-Chest-X-ray dataset (see Chapter \ref{ch:data} for more details) and the Representation Autoencoder (RAE) 
\cite{zheng2025diffusiontransformersrepresentationautoencoders} framework. In this method, the latent representation of 
the images is generated with a pre-trained visual model, in our case, a DINOv2 with registers model 
\cite{oquab2024dinov2learningrobustvisual,darcet2024visiontransformersneedregisters}. The authors trained a decoder model 
to build an autoencoder model based on a large pre-trained visual model, and the generated latents have higher semantic 
meaning and even better reconstruction quality compared to SD-VAE latents used in SOTA Stable Diffusion models. Then, they 
decided to train a flow matching model on the generated RAE latents using a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) with some specific 
architectural modification of the to be adapted to the high-dimensional RAE latents. The main inspiration for the architectural 
modification is from Decoupled Diffusion Transformer (DDT) models, to avoid the scaling of the width in the entire backbone model. 
The model was trained in ImageNet and achieve SOTA performance in FID and IS metrics compared to other pixel and latent diffusion 
models. \\
\\
Because of the impresive generation quality of the model presented in the RAE paper and the quality and utility of the RAE latents, 
I decided to train a similar model on the NIH-Chest-X-ray dataset. I trained a DiT$^{DH}$-XL (DINOv2-B) model, which consist of a
